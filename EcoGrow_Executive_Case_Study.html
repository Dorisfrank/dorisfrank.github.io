The brief GreenSphere gave, and what it actually needed

The brief named delivery mechanics, Jira, three two-week sprints, but not delivery governance. Establishing who the engagement answers to and how decisions get made was the first output, not an input.

Scattered information

Gardeners had no single, trusted source for plant-care guidance, just fragmented forums and inconsistent advice.

No real community

Gardening communities existed on generic platforms with no structure around plant type, season, or region.

Invisible progress

Nothing captured the actual rhythm of a gardening lifecycle, so there was no way to track or celebrate it.

The gap nobody had scoped: Customer Satisfaction

Power BI planning surfaced a real hole, satisfaction was a stated reporting metric with no collection mechanism anywhere upstream. Rather than bolt on a separate survey, I extended the existing usage-event infrastructure with a single-tap, post-harvest rating, scoped narrowly so it didn't expand MVP surface area this late in planning.

Stated plainly, not rounded up: Sprint 1 closed with two stories reaching Done and one carried forward. Evidence has since been captured across Sprint 2 and Sprint 3 for the remaining MVP stories, not just Sprint 1. Formal Sprint Review, final RTM validation, and full MVP acceptance remain open governance gates.

Every prototype carries an explicit disclaimer distinguishing a portfolio test build from production evidence, and none of it is represented as more than what it demonstrates.

The gate count reflects real risk, not a generic checklist applied uniformly. I set F03 and F06 to the heaviest bar because a wrong answer there could mean bad advice on a living plant or a scale commitment made on evidence that was never gathered.

F05's gates are entirely about consent and data minimisation, because personalisation earns nothing if it's not trusted first. I built none of the six from a generic checklist, each gate structure came from what that specific capability actually risks.

How I held the line between real and planned

Every deliverable across both phases, MVP and future-state alike, went through independent cross-artifact verification against the underlying source data before I called anything final.

That meant checking claims against the real Jira export, the governance registers, and each other, not accepting a status because it read well. Where something didn't hold up, it got corrected and re-verified before moving forward, and where a governance question genuinely had no answer yet, it stayed open rather than being quietly resolved for the sake of a tidier report.

The investment framework, built and still empty by design

I built the Investment and ROI Business Case framework as the next decision layer for EcoGrow's future-state roadmap, a full cost model, benefit model, adoption scenarios, and ROI and payback methodology.

Every input across the model remains genuinely open. F06's own discovery named cost drivers, adoption scenarios, and business-case evidence as unresolved questions, and explicitly ruled out an ROI claim the evidence doesn't support. The formulas are built and guarded against blank and invalid inputs, but no investment amount, cost figure, or ROI percentage has been entered, because none of that evidence exists yet.

Payback is shown as not recoverable when the investment would take longer than the defined analysis window to break even.

The model traces back to the same four business objectives already measured in the MVP, rather than treating investment analysis as a separate financial exercise. One benefit, Community Forum response, has no route into the financial model yet, because that capability itself remains deferred to Phase 2.

Model integrity, not just model existence

Building a formula isn't the same as building a correct one, and I treated them as separate checks.

A payback formula that references the right assumption cell but the wrong column still produces an error the moment real numbers land in it. A net benefit formula that quietly mixes a one-time cost with a monthly rate is wrong even when every guard clause looks right.

I checked the actual arithmetic behind each formula against what the model claims to calculate, not just whether it ran without error, before calling any of it final.

What this engagement demonstrates

Requirements traceability

Every requirement, epic, feature, and story carries an ID that traces backward to a business objective and forward into delivery evidence.

Stakeholder governance

A confidentiality-safe, persona-based Stakeholder Register and RACI, built from a genuine gap analysis, not a template.

Risk-proportionate scoping

Governance bar scaled to actual risk across six future capabilities, not a single checklist applied uniformly.

Evidence-based validation

Story-level acceptance evidence kept explicitly distinct from formal MVP acceptance, with open gates named rather than hidden.

Root-cause analysis

Each stated problem run through 5 Whys before being turned into scope, surfacing distinctions a surface read would have missed.

BI reporting design

A Power BI framework that reports the measurement structure honestly pre-launch, and only shows results once real data exists.

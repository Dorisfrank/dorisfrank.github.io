01. Project Initiation and Stakeholder Mapping

Executive Overview

Why are we doing this project?

Initial Project Understanding

Healthcare organizations rarely struggle because they lack systems. They struggle because critical business processes span multiple systems that were never designed to work together. IntelliMed Health is no exception.

Across its three hospital locations, patient records, billing, HR, and inventory each run on disconnected platforms. Staff manually transfer information between these systems, increasing the likelihood of data inconsistency, delaying financial reporting, contributing to inventory stockouts, and making compliance audits difficult to complete efficiently.

As regulatory expectations have increased, this fragmentation has become more than an operational inconvenience. Operating across UK, US, and EU contexts, IntelliMed must produce accurate, auditable information under HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital simultaneously. Fragmented systems make that difficult to sustain, which turns an IT problem into a compliance risk. A unified ERP system becomes the enabling response to that risk, not the objective in itself.

My role in this engagement is to lead the discovery and documentation phase, before implementation begins. This engagement is structured around defined stakeholder personas representing key roles, CFO, Head Nurse, IT Director, and Compliance Officer. Every stakeholder insight in this case study comes from structured elicitation against those personas, documented and analysed the same way a live engagement would require.

Engagement Objectives

This discovery engagement seeks to:

  • Understand IntelliMed's current operating model across clinical, financial, and administrative functions
  • Identify the business problems preventing efficient service delivery
  • Capture stakeholder needs across clinical, financial, compliance, and operational roles
  • Assess the gap between current capabilities and ERP functionality
  • Establish traceability from business need to proposed solution
  • Build the business case for ERP adoption
Executive Takeaway

Selecting an ERP is not the challenge.

Ensuring the selected ERP solves the right business problems for the people who use it is.

Consultant's Observation: I placed Compliance in the high-power quadrant despite holding no budget authority. HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital obligations mean the Compliance Officer cannot approve the investment, but can stop an implementation that fails to meet regulatory requirements. In a healthcare ERP engagement, that is real power, not influence in name only.

Engagement Strategy by Stakeholder Group

Manage closely (CEO, CFO, Head Nurse, IT Director, Compliance Officer): direct, decision-focused engagement through structured elicitation sessions. Output: requirements, decisions, and formal approval.

Keep satisfied (Regulatory Authorities): informational engagement, escalated only where a specific requirement carries compliance risk. Output: compliance confirmation on regulated requirements.

Keep informed (HR Manager, Procurement Lead, Clinicians, Billing Staff, ERP Vendor): structured requirements sessions and workflow validation rather than executive-level interviews. Output: workflow validation and process review.

Monitor (Patient Representative): patient impact tracked as a success measure in the business case rather than gathered through direct consultation. Output: patient-impact criteria embedded in the business case.

Success Criteria

How do we know discovery is complete?

This discovery phase will be considered complete when:

  • Stakeholder requirements are fully documented
  • Current-state workflows are validated
  • Business problems are agreed across departments
  • Requirements are prioritized
  • Traceability is established from requirement to ERP feature to business objective
  • Leadership has sufficient evidence to approve ERP investment

Executive Close

What should leadership remember before moving on?

I identified twelve stakeholder categories, five of them the defined personas this engagement structures its elicitation around.

The power-interest mapping surfaces a tension worth carrying forward deliberately: the highest-power group includes financial, clinical, technical, and regulatory owners whose requirements will not always agree. That tension becomes the central question of the next phase, understanding where business priorities align, where they conflict, and which needs must take precedence before requirements can be defined.

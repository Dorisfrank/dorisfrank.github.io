Methodology: Requirements Engineering, Gap Analysis, RTM, ERP Evaluation

01Stakeholder mapping

Twelve stakeholders identified across the engagement, five of them defined personas, CFO, Head Nurse, IT Director, Compliance Officer, and the executive sponsor, structuring the elicitation that follows in Section 03.

High power, low interestRegulatory Authorities
Low power, high interestHR Manager, Procurement Lead, Clinicians, Billing Staff, ERP Vendor
Low power, low interestPatient Representative
Consultant's Observation I placed Compliance in the high-power quadrant despite holding no budget authority. HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital obligations mean the Compliance Officer cannot approve the investment, but can stop an implementation that fails to meet regulatory requirements. In a healthcare ERP engagement, that is real power, not influence in name only.

02Needs assessment

Six departments each experience the same underlying problem differently, delayed finance reporting, fragmented patient records, unverifiable compliance, inconsistent inventory, disconnected HR scheduling, and duplicated administrative effort. All six trace back to one structural cause: four core systems that were never designed to share data.

Consultant's Observation I found that every one of these pain points traces back to the same root cause, four systems that were never designed to share data. Finance's reporting delay, Clinical's fragmented records, and Compliance's audit burden are not six separate problems requiring six separate fixes. They are one structural problem, surfacing six different ways depending on which department feels it.

Consultant's Observation I noticed three of the four personas independently described the same underlying need in different language, a single, consolidated, real-time source of truth. They are one requirement, viewed through four departmental lenses.

Consultant's Observation I found that four of the six gaps are not purely functional, they are integration gaps wearing a functional label. IntelliMed does not lack the ability to track inventory, report finances, or manage patient records. It lacks the ability to do any of those things once, in one place, and trust the result everywhere else.

09Final submission and presentation

TopicKey Message
Business ProblemFragmented systems are creating operational inefficiencies, compliance risk, and limited visibility
Root CauseFour disconnected core systems with no integrated source of truth
Discovery OutcomeRequirements, workflows, gaps, governance, and business rules documented, prioritized, and traced end to end
Recommended Next StepApprove the BRD, then proceed to structured vendor evaluation with Infor CloudSuite Healthcare and Microsoft Dynamics 365
Expected Business ValueImproved operational efficiency, stronger regulatory compliance, and a single trusted source of data visibility
Executive Takeaway

This engagement did not conclude that IntelliMed needs an ERP system.

It concluded that IntelliMed needs one trustworthy source of truth, and an ERP is the mechanism, not the goal.

06. End-to-End Requirements Traceability Matrix

Executive Overview

Can every requirement in this engagement be traced back to why it exists, and forward to what resolves it?

A requirement that cannot be traced back to a business objective is an assumption wearing a requirement's clothing. A requirement that cannot be traced forward to a gap, a formal document, and a real evaluation is a record, not a decision tool. This section builds that chain in both directions, back to Section 01's stakeholder register, forward through Section 05's gap analysis, Section 07's BRD, and Section 08's ERP evaluation, for every requirement, rule, and non-functional condition captured in Section 03.

I did not create a new prioritization or capability judgment here. Every column in this matrix pulls directly from a section already built. Where a gap in that earlier work surfaced during this exercise, I am stating it directly rather than filling it in silently.

Requirements Lifecycle

This matrix formalizes traceability through Gap Analysis. I built it as one stage in a longer lifecycle. Both stages ahead of it, the BRD and the ERP evaluation, are now complete, and the BRD Section and ERP Evaluation columns above reflect their actual content, not a placeholder:

Executive Takeaway

Fourteen requirements, rules, and conditions. Every one traces back to a named stakeholder and forward to either a gap or an explicit note on what remains unresolved.

Nothing in this engagement exists without a reason attached to it, and nothing is marked complete before it actually is.

Executive Close

What should leadership remember before moving on?

I established full traceability on all six functional requirements and all three business rules.

All five non-functional requirements now carry a MoSCoW priority, three of them newly prioritized after this exercise exposed a gap in Section 03. All five were comparatively assessed across four platforms in Section 08, though final technical validation against a specific vendor's actual documentation still awaits real procurement, not this case study.

This matrix now completes the traceability chain I built across the IntelliMed engagement. Every requirement can be followed from the business problem that created it, through workflow analysis and gap assessment, into the Business Requirements Document, and finally into ERP platform evaluation. No requirement stands alone, and none exists without a documented business rationale.

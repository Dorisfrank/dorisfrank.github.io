05. Gap Analysis

Executive Overview

Where does IntelliMed's current state fall short of what it actually needs?

Section 03 established what IntelliMed requires. Section 04 established how IntelliMed currently operates. This section holds the two against each other, requirement by requirement, to identify exactly where the gap sits, and what kind of gap it is.

I assessed each functional requirement against three questions: what can IntelliMed's current systems do today, what does the requirement actually demand, and does the shortfall come from missing functionality, missing integration between systems, or missing compliance capability. Those three gap types require different fixes, and treating them as one undifferentiated problem risks solving the wrong one.

Functional and Integration Gaps

I set severity directly from Section 03's MoSCoW ranking rather than assigning a new scale: every Must Have requirement with an unaddressed gap is rated High, every Should Have is rated Medium. A gap that duplicates a priority already established is not a new judgment, it is the same judgment applied consistently.

Compliance Gap Assessment

Compliance carries its own category because the cost of getting it wrong is different in kind, not just degree, from an operational inconvenience.

IntelliMed's current compliance reporting process, mapped in Section 04, can produce an audit response, but it cannot prove that response is complete. Each of the four source systems is reformatted and cross-checked by hand, which means the audit trail documents what staff assembled, not what actually exists across the organization. Under HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital simultaneously, that distinction is the gap: IntelliMed does not currently have a compliance problem in the sense of failing an audit. It has a compliance risk in the sense of being unable to demonstrate, with confidence, that it would pass one.

FR-06's access control gap compounds this. Compliance reporting depends on knowing exactly who accessed what patient or financial data, and when. Four independently managed access systems make that a reconciliation exercise rather than a lookup.

Non-Functional Gap Considerations

The non-functional requirements set in Section 03, uptime, encryption, response time, auditability, usability, describe qualities of a target system, not qualities I can currently assess against IntelliMed's infrastructure. Section 04's discovery mapped business process, not technical architecture, and I do not have current system specifications to compare against NFR targets with any real basis.

Rather than estimate infrastructure gaps I cannot verify, I am flagging this directly: technical fit against the non-functional requirements needs to be assessed during ERP vendor evaluation in Section 08, against each platform's actual documented specifications, not assumed here.

Capability Assessment

Mapping the six requirement gaps back to the module structure established in Section 03 gives a clear picture of where platform capability matters most:

  • Patient Management: must resolve FR-01, the highest-severity gap in this analysis
  • Billing and Finance: must resolve FR-02
  • Inventory: must resolve FR-03
  • Audit and Compliance: must resolve FR-04, and depends on System-wide access control being resolved first
  • HR: must resolve FR-05
  • System-wide: must resolve FR-06, which several other modules depend on functioning correctly

Gap Summary

Consultant's Observation: I found that four of the six gaps are not purely functional, they are integration gaps wearing a functional label. IntelliMed does not lack the ability to track inventory, report finances, or manage patient records. It lacks the ability to do any of those things once, in one place, and trust the result everywhere else. A platform that adds features without solving integration would leave the actual gap untouched.

Executive Close

What should leadership remember before moving on?

Executive Takeaway

IntelliMed is not choosing an ERP to gain capabilities it doesn't have.

It is choosing an ERP to make the capabilities it already has trustworthy in one place instead of scattered across four.

Six gaps identified, four of them integration-driven rather than purely functional, and one compliance risk that exists independent of whether any individual audit has ever failed.

01

Project initiation & discovery

Complete

GreenSphere's brief specified delivery mechanics, Jira, three two-week sprints, but not delivery governance. Before any backlog or sprint work began, I established who the engagement answers to, how decisions get made, and what problem is actually being solved. Given confidentiality on this live engagement, stakeholder engagement throughout this report is represented through structured personas built against defined roles, not named individuals.

PersonaRolePower / Interest
Product SponsorBudget authority, Go/No-Go decisionsHigh / High

Scrum MasterCeremony facilitation, cadenceMedium / Medium

Full Stakeholder Register, Power-Interest analysis, RACI, and Communication Plan in Section 1 and the Excel Governance Pack.

02

Business analysis & problem definition

Complete

I root-caused GreenSphere's three stated problems, scattered information, lack of community, ineffective tracking, independently using a 5 Whys technique rather than taking them at face value. Each traces to a distinct business objective.

IDObjectiveRoot cause addressed
BO-01Trusted, horticulture-specific information sourceNo platform has positioned itself as community-vetted
BO-02Structured space to connect and exchange knowledgeCommunity exists but lacks domain-specific structure
BO-03Simple way to track progress and recognise achievementNo tool matches the actual gardening lifecycle
BO-04Capture usage data for future decisionsBusiness rationale stated in the original brief
03

Requirements engineering

Complete

I traced nine functional and four non-functional requirements directly to the four business objectives above, each prioritised using MoSCoW and mapped for dependencies before any backlog work began. I added FR-09 mid-build after Power BI planning surfaced a real gap, Customer Satisfaction had no defined collection mechanism anywhere upstream.

Full requirements catalogue, acceptance criteria, and traceability in Section 3 and the RTM tab of the Excel Governance Pack.

04

Product strategy & delivery planning

Complete

I scoped the MVP to address all three of GreenSphere's stated problems within the brief's three-sprint constraint, deliberately sequencing Community Forum response into Phase 2 rather than compressing full community functionality into the MVP.

EpicMVP scope
Plant LibraryFull, search and vetted content
Harvest TrackerFull, planting and harvest logging
Community ForumPosting only, response deferred to Phase 2
Usage Analytics CaptureFull, extended to include satisfaction capture (FR-09)
05

Backlog engineering

Complete

I sequenced seven user stories by the gardener's actual journey, find, plant, share, rather than by epic in isolation, with dependencies explicitly mapped so Sprint 1 planning doesn't stall on a blocked story.

Full story list, dependency matrix, and story mapping in Section 5, ready to load directly into Jira per the Jira Build Specification.

06

Sprint planning & agile delivery

Real evidence, Sprint 1 through Sprint 3

I closed Sprint 1 against available Jira evidence. Sprint 2 evidence was captured for ECO-7, ECO-8, and ECO-10, and Sprint 3 evidence was captured for ECO-9, the ECO-10 follow-up, and ECO-12. No scope changes, no work completed outside a sprint. Velocity and story points remain genuine gaps in the Jira data, not withheld numbers.

Full Sprint 1 evidence table, risk log, and Sprint 2/3 status in Section 6.

07–08

Business validation & release

Complete, governance gates open
I kept Section 7's story-level acceptance evidence for all seven MVP stories explicitly distinct from formal MVP acceptance. Section 8 defines the release readiness checklist and KPI framework. Formal all-persona Sprint Review, final RTM validation, and Go/No-Go approval remain the genuinely open governance gates, I'm naming them here rather than closing them for a tidier page.

Full frameworks in Sections 7 and 8.

09

Executive delivery summary & next steps

Complete

I built the full report to run end to end, from GreenSphere's three originally stated problems through requirements, backlog, real sprint delivery evidence, business validation, and release readiness. Formal Sprint Review, RTM validation, and MVP acceptance remain the outstanding governance gates before go-live.

03. Requirements Elicitation

Executive Overview

What does the business actually need, and how was that captured?

Section 02 established that IntelliMed's six departmental symptoms trace back to one structural problem, systems that cannot share data. This section turns that problem into requirements a solution can actually be built against.

Elicitation Approach

I captured requirements through structured elicitation against four defined stakeholder personas, CFO, Head Nurse, IT Director, and Compliance Officer, representing the roles with the highest combined power and interest identified in Section 01.

I drew on four inputs:

  • Structured elicitation against stakeholder personas, using a consistent four-part format for each role
  • Document review of the original project brief, business challenge, and rationale
  • Domain research into ERP adoption patterns in healthcare, including the NHS, Kaiser Permanente, Charité, and Apollo Hospitals examples cited in the project rationale
  • Cross-reference against Section 01's stakeholder register, so each requirement ties back to a documented business objective rather than being captured in isolation
Technique Why It Was Used
Structured elicitation against stakeholder personas Capture business need directly from the role that owns it
Document review Validate findings against the original project brief and rationale
Domain research Benchmark against real healthcare ERP adoption patterns
Cross-reference to stakeholder register Ensure every requirement traces to a documented business objective, not an isolated request

Stakeholder Perspectives

How each stakeholder's role shaped the requirements that follow.

CFO
Business Objective Financial visibility and budget control
Current Challenge Month-end close requires manual reconciliation across three disconnected billing and accounting systems
Key Requirement Real-time, consolidated financial reporting across all three hospital locations
Business Rationale Reduces reporting cycle time and removes manual reconciliation as a source of error before figures reach leadership

Head Nurse
Business Objective Clinical workflow efficiency
Current Challenge Patient records are not unified across locations, so a patient treated at more than one site does not have a single continuous record
Key Requirement A unified patient record accessible in real time across all three hospital locations
Business Rationale Reduces the risk of clinical decisions being made on incomplete information

IT Director
Business Objective Systems integration and security
Current Challenge No integration layer exists between patient records, billing, HR, and inventory systems
Key Requirement A single ERP platform with secure, role-based access across all four core functions
Business Rationale Removes manual data transfer as a source of error and creates one point of security control instead of four

Compliance Officer
Business Objective Regulatory compliance across HIPAA, GDPR, NHS Digital
Current Challenge Audit-ready reporting must be manually assembled from four separate systems, and completeness cannot be easily proven
Key Requirement Automated, auditable reporting that draws from a single consolidated data source
Business Rationale Converts compliance reporting from a manual assembly exercise into a repeatable, defensible process
Consultant's Observation: I noticed three of the four personas independently described the same underlying need in different language, a single, consolidated, real-time source of truth. The CFO calls it financial visibility. The Head Nurse calls it a unified patient record. The Compliance Officer calls it an auditable data source. These are not four different requirements. They are one requirement, viewed through four departmental lenses.

Requirements Catalogue

What the ERP system must actually do, and under what conditions.

Functional Requirements

ID Requirement Module Source Stakeholder
FR-01 Consolidate patient records across all three locations into a single accessible record Patient Management Head Nurse
FR-02 Provide real-time financial reporting across all hospital locations Billing and Finance CFO
FR-03 Provide real-time inventory tracking with automated low-stock alerts Inventory Procurement Lead
FR-04 Generate audit-ready compliance reports across HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital without manual assembly Audit and Compliance Compliance Officer
FR-05 Integrate HR scheduling data with clinical and administrative demand HR HR Manager
FR-06 Provide role-based access control across all four core modules System-wide IT Director

FR-03 and FR-05 trace to Procurement Lead and HR Manager, stakeholders outside the four core personas. Both are captured through the cross-reference to Section 01's stakeholder register described above, not through direct elicitation, consistent with how this section's methodology is scoped.

Non-Functional Requirements

ID Requirement Category
NFR-01 System uptime of 99.5% or higher during operating hours Reliability
NFR-02 Patient data encrypted at rest and in transit Security
NFR-03 Role-based access must be auditable and independently verifiable Compliance
NFR-04 Core workflows (patient lookup, billing entry) must complete in under 3 seconds Performance
NFR-05 Interface must be usable by clinical staff with minimal additional training Usability

Business Rules

  • BR-01: Patient data may only be accessed by staff with an active clinical or administrative assignment to that patient
  • BR-02: Financial reports must reconcile automatically against source billing data before publication to leadership
  • BR-03: Inventory reorder thresholds must be configurable per supply category, not fixed system-wide

Assumptions

  • Stakeholder personas represent the actual operational priorities of their respective roles
  • Existing hospital infrastructure can support a modern ERP platform without a separate infrastructure upgrade project
  • Staff will receive adequate training and change management support during rollout

Constraints

  • Implementation must comply with HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital simultaneously, not sequentially
  • The solution must integrate with, or replace, four existing legacy systems without a full service interruption
  • Budget and timeline are to be defined during the business case phase, not assumed at the requirements stage

Executive Takeaway

Four stakeholder perspectives highlighted different business priorities.

Underneath the language, they were asking for the same thing: one trustworthy source of truth the whole hospital can act on.

Executive Close

What should leadership remember before moving on?

Six requirements, five non-functional conditions, three business rules, and a MoSCoW ranking that puts patient safety and regulatory exposure ahead of operational convenience. Nothing here stands alone.

Each requirement carries its stakeholder and business objective forward into the Requirements Traceability Matrix. The prioritized catalogue becomes the basis for the Gap Analysis. The BRD that closes this engagement will formalize what has already been discovered, validated, and prioritized here, not invent it fresh. The next section maps how IntelliMed's current workflows actually operate today, the step that determines whether these requirements are a redesign of existing process or a genuine departure from it.

Appendix B: Elicitation Question Guide

The following questions represent the guide used to structure elicitation against each persona. They are the questions asked of the discovery process, not transcribed responses.

CFO: How is financial reporting currently consolidated across locations? Where do delays or reconciliation errors most often originate? What financial visibility is missing today that leadership needs?

Head Nurse: How are patient records currently accessed across the three hospital locations? Where does incomplete information create clinical risk? What would a unified record need to support at the point of care?

IT Director: Where do the current systems fail to integrate? What security and access-control gaps exist today? What technical constraints would shape an ERP rollout?

Compliance Officer: How is audit-ready reporting currently assembled? Where is compliance across HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital hardest to demonstrate? What would make an audit trail defensible without manual assembly?

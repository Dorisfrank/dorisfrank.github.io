IntelliMed Health: Business Requirements Document

BRD Section 1: Introduction and Purpose

This document defines the business requirements for an ERP implementation at IntelliMed Health, a three-location hospital network currently operating patient records, billing, HR, and inventory as four disconnected systems. It is intended to give leadership a single, traceable reference for what the organization needs from an ERP platform, and why, before any vendor is evaluated.

BRD Section 2: Business Need and Problem Statement

IntelliMed Health operates four core business systems that do not communicate with one another. This fragmentation forces staff to transfer data manually between systems, which delays financial reporting, obscures real-time inventory visibility, complicates regulatory compliance across HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital, and prevents leadership from making decisions based on a single, trusted view of hospital operations.

The problem is not a lack of data. IntelliMed generates more than enough data across its departments to run effectively. The problem is that none of it is structured to be shared, trusted, or acted on quickly.

BRD Section 2.5: Business Objectives

The ERP implementation seeks to:

  • Integrate patient records, billing, HR, and inventory data onto a single integrated platform
  • Reduce the financial reporting cycle from manual, multi-system reconciliation to a real-time consolidated view
  • Provide real-time inventory visibility sufficient to prevent critical stockouts before they occur
  • Generate audit-ready compliance reporting across HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital without manual assembly
  • Give leadership a single, trusted operational view across all three hospital locations

These objectives carry forward directly from the Business Goals established in Section 02. No new objective is introduced here that wasn't already stated there.

BRD Section 3: Project Scope and Boundaries

In scope: - Consolidation of patient records, billing, HR, and inventory data onto a single platform - Real-time financial and inventory reporting - Automated compliance reporting across HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital - Unified role-based access control

Out of scope: - Selection of a specific ERP vendor (addressed in Section 08 of this case study, not this document) - Infrastructure procurement - Detailed technical architecture design - Clinical protocol changes unrelated to data access

BRD Section 6: Non-Functional Requirements

These requirements define how the ERP solution must perform rather than what it must do.

ID Requirement Category
NFR-01 System uptime of 99.5% or higher during operating hours Reliability
NFR-02 Patient data encrypted at rest and in transit Security
NFR-03 Role-based access must be auditable and independently verifiable Compliance
NFR-04 Core workflows must complete in under 3 seconds Performance
NFR-05 Interface must be usable by clinical staff with minimal additional training Usability

BRD Section 7: Business Rules

The following business rules govern how the solution must operate regardless of the ERP platform selected.

  • BR-01: Patient data may only be accessed by staff with an active clinical or administrative assignment to that patient
  • BR-02: Financial reports must reconcile automatically against source billing data before publication to leadership
  • BR-03: Inventory reorder thresholds must be configurable per supply category, not fixed system-wide

BRD Section 8: Assumptions and Constraints

The following assumptions and constraints define the boundaries within which these requirements were developed, and should be revisited if any of them change materially before implementation.

Assumptions: stakeholder personas represent the actual operational priorities of their respective roles; existing hospital infrastructure can support a modern ERP platform without a separate infrastructure upgrade project; staff will receive adequate training and change management support during rollout.

Constraints: implementation must comply with HIPAA, GDPR, and NHS Digital simultaneously, not sequentially; the solution must integrate with, or replace, four existing legacy systems without a full service interruption; budget and timeline are to be defined during the business case phase, not assumed at the requirements stage.

BRD Section 9: Approval and Sign-off

This is a draft, presented for stakeholder review. No formal approval has been recorded against this document. Sign-off authority sits with the CEO, CFO, and Compliance Officer jointly, consistent with their designation as high-power, high-interest stakeholders in Section 01.

BRD Section 10: Acceptance and Success Criteria

This BRD will be considered complete and actionable when:

  • Stakeholder requirements are fully documented (complete, see Section 03)
  • Requirements are formally approved by the sign-off authority named in Section 9 (pending)
  • Traceability is established from requirement to gap to business objective (complete, see Section 06)
  • ERP platform evaluation is completed against these requirements (pending, see Section 08)
  • Leadership approval is obtained to proceed with implementation (pending)

Executive Takeaway

A Business Requirements Document is not where analysis happens.

It is where analysis already done gets formalized into something an organization can act on and be held to.

I built this BRD to formalize the outputs of IntelliMed's discovery phase into a single decision-ready document. Every requirement, business rule, assumption, and constraint in it traces back to analysis already completed earlier in this engagement.

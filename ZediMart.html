01Project overview

ZediMart is a fast-growing retail and distribution business operating ten stores across five Nigerian cities, Lagos, Port Harcourt, Abuja, Ibadan, and Kano. Leadership needed a consolidated, trustworthy view of customer behavior, order validity, and inventory health to support operational, financial, and marketing decisions, but the underlying data was spread across six disconnected tables with no confirmed relationship between them.

I was asked to answer four specific stakeholder questions: build a unified customer list for Marketing, validate order and payment data for Finance, reconcile inventory against the product catalogue for Operations, and produce a clean, executive-ready revenue report. Rather than answering each question at face value, I tested the evidence behind every answer before reporting it.

Guiding principle: reliable decisions begin with validated evidence. Each section below is either a direct confirmation of data integrity, or an honest statement of what the data cannot yet confirm.

Order value brackets: Low 477, Medium 307, High 216. And the headline finding: Sales (Nov 2024 to Apr 2025, ₦7,447,707) and Orders (all of 2026, ₦10,447,947) share zero overlapping days. A naive combined total of ₦17,895,654 would misrepresent two periods that never coexisted.

Part 3: Inventory evidence qualified by schema limitations

No field in the Products table confirms discontinued status. The only honest proxy, zero stock across every store, returns zero candidates. What the data can confirm instead: 154 of 1,000 store-product combinations sit below their defined reorder level, six of them at zero stock right now.

Part 4: Executive reporting built on established meaning

523 of 1,000 orders fall above the dataset average, cleanly cast and free of NULLs. And the capstone query makes the Sales/Orders risk visible directly inside the reporting layer, rather than letting it happen silently inside a dashboard visual.

05Business recommendations

Seven recommendations, each derived from evidence confirmed during this analysis, rather than predetermined actions inherited from the original case study brief.

1. Formalize the validated customer list as the single source of truth

Traces to: O4, Query 1, Query 2
What I analysed: all 500 customer records for duplication and completeness.
What I found: zero duplication, zero missing values.
Business insightThe brief assumed this data needed deduplication. It did not. The more valuable outcome is confirmation that every department can already work from one shared list without drift over time.
Recommendation: adopt this list as the single reference customer table across departments.
Success metric: zero variance between departmental customer counts.

2. Report Sales and Orders as two separate, clearly labeled figures

Traces to: R1, O1, Query 7, Query 12
What I found: zero overlapping days between the two revenue-bearing tables.
Business insightThe natural instinct on any dashboard is to sum every revenue table into one headline number. Here, that instinct produces a figure that is technically additive but not meaningfully true.
Recommendation: report the two figures separately until the relationship is confirmed with source systems.

3. Escalate the future-dated orders pattern

Traces to: R2, Query 7 (date range finding)
What I found: 413 of 1,000 orders, 41.3%, carry a date later than the analysis date.
Business insightThis affects nearly half the Orders table. "Current order volume" cannot be answered confidently until the cause is understood.
Recommendation: raise directly with the Orders source system owner before reporting current-period metrics.

4. Restock immediately, then review restocking cadence

Traces to: O3, R3, Query 10
What I found: 6 combinations at zero stock, 154 below reorder level, concentrated at 5 stores led by Branch 9 in Kano.
Business insightThe 6 zero-stock items are an isolated fix. The broader pattern, concentrated in five specific stores, is a process problem, not six unrelated stockouts.
Recommendation: restock immediately, then commission a structural cadence review at the five highest-breach stores.

5. Add a product lifecycle status field to the schema

Traces to: O5, R4, Query 9
What I found: no field distinguishes active from discontinued products. The zero-stock proxy returns no candidates.
Business insightThis is a genuine data model gap, not a data quality issue. No amount of further querying answers this with certainty.
Recommendation: add a status field before this question is asked again.

6. Build a tiered engagement strategy around the High-value segment

Traces to: O2, Query 6
What I found: 216 orders, 21.6% of volume, generate 37.0% of total order revenue.
Business insightThe highest-value fifth of orders produces more than a third of revenue on its own, worth protecting deliberately rather than leaving to chance.
Recommendation: identify these customers and build a retention strategy specifically around them.

7. Confirm whether an unpaid payment status exists upstream

Traces to: R5, Query 5
What I found: every order carries a completed payment method. No pending status exists anywhere in this table.
Business insightEither ZediMart genuinely has no incomplete transactions, unusual for a retailer this size, or they exist upstream and simply aren't flowing into this data.
Recommendation: confirm directly with the source payment system before Finance treats this data as complete.

06Implementation roadmap

Four phases, sequenced by actual dependency, not convenience.

Phase 1: Immediate fixes

Week 1 · Depends on nothing already verified

Restock 6 zero-stock items. Separate Sales/Orders reporting. Hand the High-value segment to Marketing. Update the data dictionary and header-row documentation.

Phase 2: Investigation and escalation

Weeks 2–6 · Depends on response time from other teams

Escalate the future-dated orders pattern. Confirm the unpaid-status question with the payment system. Begin data gathering for the restocking cadence review.

Phase 3: Structural change

Months 2–4 · Content deliberately conditional on Phase 2's findings

Add the product status field. Implement restocking changes based on what Phase 2 uncovers. Resolve the Sales/Orders relationship permanently, formalize separation or build corrected join logic, whichever the evidence supports.

Phase 4: Validation and monitoring

Month 3 onward, ongoing

Quarterly customer audits. Track the High-value segment's revenue share over time. Monitor reorder breaches monthly across all ten stores. Re-verify no combined revenue figure has quietly reappeared.

08Executive conclusion

This engagement began with four stakeholder questions. The analysis uncovered something more consequential than direct answers to them.

Several assumptions in the original brief did not survive contact with the data. The customer base did not need the deduplication it was assumed to need. Whether products were discontinued could not be confirmed at all, not because the answer was hidden, but because the data model was never built to answer that question. Whether every order was truly paid could not be confirmed either, for the same reason.

None of these were failures of analysis. They were the analysis working correctly, testing an assumption rather than accepting it, and reporting the honest answer even when the honest answer was that the data cannot say.

What changed as a result: leadership now has a revenue reporting structure that will not silently misstate two disconnected periods as one continuous figure. Five stores know exactly where their restocking gaps are concentrated, led by Kano's Branch 9. A ₦3.86M revenue segment has a name and a strategy instead of disappearing into an average. And two real gaps in the data model itself are now documented decisions rather than invisible blind spots.

Reliable decisions begin with validated evidence. That principle shaped every finding, every recommendation, and every honest limitation stated on this page.
